Closet math

A closet organizer bought by eye means a rail that fits fewer shirts than the shop photo promised and shelves that stop short of the top. Closet math turns the rail length and a labeled width an item into the wardrobe count, turns the rail and the level count into the total hanging, and turns the column height and a labeled spacing into the shelf count. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Rail plan - the rail length at a labeled width an item becomes the hanging count and the spare centimeters.
  • Level plan - the rail times the level count becomes the total hanging in the column.
  • Shelf plan - the column height at a labeled spacing becomes the shelf count and the gap at the top.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: 3 cm of rail for each hanging item, 30 cm of spacing for each shelf - all illustrative defaults you can change. Wardrobes vary from silk shirts to winter coats, and organizers rate their own rails and fixings. This is planning arithmetic, not a fitting: measure the real closet and count the real clothes.
